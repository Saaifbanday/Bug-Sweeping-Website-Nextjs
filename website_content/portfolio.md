His work covers:

* Missing-person tracing
* Technical surveillance counter-measures

On 5 September 2026 the World Association of Detectives named him Investigator of the Year at its 101st Annual Conference in Cannes, France.

### Philosophy

Most matters that reach him are not mysteries. They are decisions somebody is about to make — a hire, a marriage, an acquisition, a dismissal, a filing — where the facts are incomplete and being wrong is expensive.

The work is to close that gap carefully, inside the law, and to report what the evidence supports rather than what the client hopes for.

### Engagement philosophy

The first conversation matters.

Some enquiries can be answered in an hour of records work. Some need weeks of fieldwork. Some cannot be taken at all, because the only method that would answer them is unlawful.

The website should clearly communicate that the investigator will explain this before any engagement begins.

# 8. Method

## Section label

**05 — Method**

## Heading

**How a matter is actually run.**

Supporting text:

Five working principles decide what is accepted, how it is worked, and what the client receives at the end.

---

## I — Scope before fieldwork

The first conversation defines:

* The question
* Lawful methods available
* Likely timeline
* Cost

Nothing starts before that is agreed in writing.

---

## II — Lawful methods only

The following are not offered at any price:

* Interception
* Account access
* Tracking without authority
* Impersonation of officials

An enquiry that needs them is declined, and the reason is explained.

---

## III — Evidence over inference

Findings are separated from interpretation in the report.

Where something is probable but unproven, it is written as probable and unproven.

---

## IV — Need-to-know handling

Field operatives work from task briefs, not case histories.

Client identity, case detail and findings are shared only with the people the engagement requires.

---

## V — A report you can use

Reports should contain:

* Dated observations
* Source of each fact
* Indexed supporting material
* Clear statement of what remains unverified

Position this as a document that can survive being read by the other side.

# 16. Design Direction

The website should feel like a **premium international investigative consultancy**, not a generic detective-agency website.

Prioritize:

* Strong typography
* Editorial layout
* High-quality photography
* Generous whitespace
* Clear hierarchy
* Dark / sophisticated visual language where appropriate
* Restrained animations
* Subtle transitions
* Strong mobile responsiveness
* High readability
* Trust and discretion
* Premium visual presentation

Avoid:

* Cheap detective-agency aesthetics
* Excessive red/black "crime" styling
* Stock photos of magnifying glasses
* Cartoonish spy imagery
* Excessive animations
* Overcrowded sections
* Generic marketing clichés
* Aggressive sales language

The website should communicate **professional investigation, evidence, discretion and credibility**.

# 19. Implementation Notes for Claude Code

Before modifying the project:

1. Inspect the existing codebase and understand the current architecture.
2. Identify the framework, routing structure, styling system and reusable components.
3. Reuse existing components where appropriate.
4. Do not unnecessarily rewrite working infrastructure.
5. Keep the site responsive across desktop, tablet and mobile.
6. Ensure semantic HTML and accessibility.
7. Optimize images and loading performance.
8. Preserve clean component structure.
9. Keep content separated from presentation where practical.
10. Ensure all navigation and CTA links work.
11. Ensure the enquiry form has proper validation and user feedback.
12. Do not expose sensitive form submissions client-side.
13. Check the site for broken links, layout issues and console errors after implementation.
